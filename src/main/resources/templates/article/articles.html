What is a Borewell?

A borewell is a deep, narrow hole drilled into the ground to access groundwater for various uses like drinking, irrigation, or industrial needs. It is created using specialized drilling machines, and a pipe (called casing) is installed to protect the water source and support the structure.

Key Insight

Borewells are a primary solution in areas where surface water is limited. The depth can range from 100 to 1200 feet, depending on the underground water table. A submersible pump is typically installed inside to lift water to the surface efficiently.

Types of Borewell Drilling Methods

Selecting the right drilling method is crucial for successful borewell installation. The method depends on geological conditions, depth requirements, and budget constraints.

DTH (Down-The-Hole)

Suitable for hard rock areas, uses a hammering action to break rocks. This method is efficient for deep drilling in rocky terrain.

Best for: Hard rock formations

In-Well Method

In this method, the drilling vehicle is parked 150–200 feet away from the actual borewell location. Flexible hoses or rods are extended to the drilling spot.

Best for: Narrow plots & limited access areas

Rotary Drilling

Used in soft or sandy formations, involves continuous rotation and drilling mud for stabilization during the drilling process.

Best for: Soft soil & sandy areas

Calyx Method

An older method used for large diameter and deep wells with specific geological needs. Less common but still used in certain applications.

Best for: Large diameter wells

Choosing the Right Location

The success of a borewell largely depends on selecting a proper location. Strategic planning can prevent future issues and ensure optimal water yield.

Things to DO

  • Choose a clear and open area for drilling operations
  • Ensure sufficient space for drilling vehicles and equipment
  • Consult with a hydrogeologist for water table assessment
  • Check local regulations and obtain necessary permits

Things to AVOID

  • Avoid areas with underground chambers, pipelines, or water tanks
  • Don't drill near septic tanks or building foundations
  • Avoid low-lying areas prone to flooding
  • Stay away from contaminated soil or industrial waste areas

Important Note

Underground obstructions can damage equipment or affect borewell structure. Always conduct a thorough site survey before drilling commences. Consider using geophysical methods to identify underground structures and water-bearing zones.

How to Choose the Right Borewell Contractor

Selecting the right contractor is critical for borewell success. Since drilling involves technical expertise and significant investment, your contractor choice directly impacts the outcome.

Contractor Selection Checklist

Experience & Expertise

An experienced contractor brings knowledge of soil types, rock layers, and water tables. Ask about years in service and request a list of local projects they've completed.

Equipment Quality

Modern, well-maintained rigs complete jobs efficiently. Check if they use rotary, percussion, or DTH rigs suitable for your soil conditions.

Reputation & Reviews

Word-of-mouth and reviews indicate reliability. Ask neighbors and check online feedback about pricing honesty, timeliness, and after-service support.

Licensing & Insurance

Ensure the contractor has proper licenses and insurance to protect against accidents or damages during drilling operations.

Transparent Pricing

A reputable contractor provides detailed quotations with clear breakdowns of costs, including drilling, casing, pump installation, and any additional charges.

Questions to Ask Your Contractor

1

What is your experience in this specific area?

2

Past clients can offer honest feedback about the contractor's work.

3
